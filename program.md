# Zenith research programme

Zenith asks whether an investigation can acquire a better way of organising the problem it is investigating. Experience should change useful distinctions, state, computations, interfaces or experiments, so later work becomes more capable or less costly. The graph is a revisable theory of that organisation. It may grow, shrink or compile into ordinary code.

The [fifteen-return synthesis](docs/deep-returns-20261014.md) makes **learning the decomposition** the next research target. This is a proposed direction. The active implementation remains one investigator, an observation ledger and source-bound program versions with optional executable dependencies. It proposes experiments, observes actual SQLite responses, writes restricted Python and retains or replaces code under a declared development rule. There is no implemented general reflective graph or demonstrated recursively improving learner.

## What the graph should learn

A node's local task can be impossible because its interface discarded information, its state forgot a relevant event, or its consumer needs a distinction it never represented. Improving the node's answer within that boundary may never fix the problem. A node with suitable surrounding context can instead propose changing the boundary.

For example, a caller passing only the low byte makes headers `0001` and `0101` indistinguishable to its helper, while their required lengths are 1 and 257. The helper must change what reaches it or narrow what it claims. This exact obstruction motivates a model experiment; it is not evidence that a model already discovered the repair. Other returned prototypes acquire a history register and its rollback lifetime, extract reusable helpers from acquired end-to-end code, or derive calibration experiments from an acquired model.

The proposed loop is: observe a limitation; propose a different representation, interface or question; investigate its consequence; retain useful knowledge and evidence that can challenge it. Several local errors may reveal one missing abstraction. A broad abstraction may instead need specialization or removal. Upward generalisation is one possible move, not the organising rule.

Nodes can initially be questions, candidate explanations or conjectures. Reflection need not immediately produce code or improve the serving score. Exploratory alternatives, partial results and regressing intermediates can be useful. Their eventual value must connect to a valid argument, independent observation or later consumer. The system may question the task's framing without silently replacing the externally accepted objective.

## What is retained

Retain executable alternatives, raw observations, counterexamples and assumptions needed to interpret them. Source identity binds bytes, not current meaning. A failed assumption can invalidate a conclusion and a reason for excluding an old alternative. Reopen affected reasoning rather than erasing the entire archive or treating component boundaries as proof of independence.

A computation dependency, evidence supporting an assumption, and an experiment that distinguishes alternatives are different relationships. They can share a small representation. A local worker needs surrounding information that changes its decision: inputs/consumers, source assumptions, failures and feasible actions, with access to the originals. It need not ingest the whole graph or run permanent reflection. Validated operations can execute as ordinary code.

The learning language and update algorithm may initially be supplied. Source-dependent acquisition within them is still learning. An ordinary-code tie limits special architectural credit; it does not erase acquired knowledge. The question is whether explicit local context improves discovery, repair or reuse at matched effort, not whether graphs express computations unavailable to flat libraries.

## Evidence so far

The [observation-learning protocol](docs/executable-investigations.md) compares incremental state with an execution-enabled archive and fresh compiler. All receive the same external evidence and six proposal calls per source. Six configured SQLite sources expose duplicate writes, triggers, rollbacks and separate connections. Native execution supplies measurements; construction freezes before protected evaluation.

The 126-call campaign cost **$0.12947251224**. Final incremental and unchanged early programs given final evidence both scored 244/288. Archive and fresh construction scored 187/288 and 230/288; source-level comparison intervals span zero. Continued revision added no measured benefit. A selected fresh DeepSeek reconstruction later confirmed at 188/288 against 23/288 for unchanged failed seeds. Complementary retained DeepSeek/Qwen proposals then confirmed at 279/288. These favour evidence retention and alternative candidate search on selected failures; they do not establish an improved learning method.

A stronger follow-on reference reached 286/288 using the full historical archive, two fixed native probes per source and no new model calls. Its search budget is larger. An authored disagreement policy improved scores in the smaller pool by avoiding a contradiction to a shared simulator error, not identifying more of the source. Independent challenges remain essential. Five composition versions stopped before protected scoring, leaving graph benefit unresolved. Different panels and budgets are not pooled into one accuracy.

The [ten-return](docs/frontier-returns-20261013.md) and [eight-return](docs/next-returns-20261014.md) assimilations retain their reproduction boundaries. The fifteen new returns were inspected for arguments, code and custody, **not rerun**. They report bounded acquisition of state, composition and experimental recipes, often tying ordinary implementations. Their combined lead is acquired structure that changes later investigation. They do not yet establish reflective model nodes, a superior graph runtime, a qualified production workload or an improving learning algorithm.

## The next test

Compare context-informed structural revision before building a larger framework. Small source adapters or software semantics with independently specified outputs are suitable initial mechanisms. Include an information-losing interface, missing historical state, a discoverable shared computation and an irrelevant or stale abstraction. Freeze new source/task pairs and later cases before proposals; do not supply the desired decomposition or rely solely on the known byte example.

The principal competitor is a strong ordinary code agent allowed to inspect the same evidence, refactor the whole program, add state/helpers and design probes. Compare it with selected nodes receiving compact caller/consumer context and permission to change boundaries. A fixed-interface condition diagnoses obstruction; an equivalent code/contracts representation tests whether special graph navigation adds anything beyond the information. Ordinary code may retain shared bodies, dispatch and state without forced duplication.

Match total inference, context retrieval and source-action budgets. Retain unsuccessful proposals and charge acquisition as well as serving. Compare changed structure with earlier structure given final evidence and fresh reconstruction from full history. A no-history snapshot separates acquisition from pretrained knowledge. Measure whole-task correctness, unsupported commitments, later construction/repair effort and full cost across source/task pairs. More edges, prose or attempts do not count as improvement.

Seek a concrete trace: a limitation causes a new distinction or interface, another computation or investigation uses it, and a later independent case benefits. Ablation tests actual use, not unique semantics. A tie with a simpler agent still permits a learning result but selects the simpler organisation. If no condition improves, diagnose information, representation and search separately.

After a positive mechanism result, test whether the acquired model generates better experiments on new bindings. For a claim about the learning method itself, transplant only the changed investigation/update rule, without old code, answers or histories; compare new acquisition with a competent pilot-tuned fixed rule. First-run structural improvement is possible and distinct from this policy-transfer claim.

## Relation to the graph ambition

The ambition remains: useful experience changes what the system can notice, ask, represent and do. The proposed graph makes these changes inspectable and locally actionable. It need not remain a graph at execution time, and every node need not be an agent. A theoretical graph can guide discovery before hypotheses are executable, provided claims eventually meet the appropriate form of evidence.

Self-editing agents and contextual feedback have substantial prior art, linked in the synthesis. The possible contribution is efficient, context-informed discovery and revision of reusable distinctions and interfaces. Effectiveness and novelty remain research questions. Historical financial and operational studies keep their original scopes; current claims are owned by the [paper](paper/zenith.tex), [status](docs/status.md) and exact experiment receipts.
