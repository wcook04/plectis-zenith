{\Large\bfseries Zenith: programs, experiments and useful memory}\\[4pt]
Will Cook \quad\textbullet\quad 15 October 2026
\end{center}
\hypertarget{idea}{}
\textbf{Abstract.}
We investigate whether retaining programs and their supporting experiments improves
performance on subsequent tasks. We evaluate three mechanisms separately:
model-driven program repair, reuse of generated testers, and an authored
continuation learner. Repair alone increases correct later answers from
259/576 to 365/576; additional investigation does not improve on this baseline.
Each of two generated testers detects three of four authored faults, whereas
fixed tests detect all four. Retained continuation tests reduce acquisition
queries in a controlled ablation, but the learner's total cost exceeds that of
ordinary alternatives. These results identify implementation requirements and
limitations; they do not establish a new learning algorithm or recursive
self-improvement. We outline experiments on transferable investigation
procedures and discuss mathematics as a possible evaluation domain.

\textbf{1. Introduction and experimental setting.}
We represent retained artifacts as $(P,E,Q)$: a program, source-bound
observations, and executable tests or distinguishing continuations. A useful addition must improve
later behavior or reduce the work required to acquire it. Cached answers,
learned representations and better investigation procedures require separate
controls. Model weights remain fixed in these studies.

Predictive state representations encode stochastic state through probabilities
of future tests. The cited theorem constructs a representation from a known
finite POMDP; it does not supply a black-box learning algorithm~\cite[Theorem 1; \S2]{psr}.
Our continuation learner instead fits exact integer observations in a
rational-linear model. The conceptual connection is the use of future tests
to distinguish histories.

\hypertarget{lab}{}
\textbf{Failure diagnosis.}
A wrong answer may reflect discarded history, an incorrect use of sufficient
state, or behavior never observed. The archived SQLite program retained pending
inserts but ignored them when reading allocation state; another rows-only helper
discarded necessary history. These need different interventions.
The records expose the distinction; they do not establish reliable autonomous
diagnosis. A model's explanation of a failure remains a hypothesis.

\hypertarget{oracle}{}
\textbf{Program repair.}
In the native study, a model receives a task contract, a whole candidate program,
examples and the measurements available to its condition. It proposes queries;
the host compares candidate and reference answers. A repair request proposes
replacement code. Selection preserves every previously correct measured case
and the aggregate development result before later panels open. Rejected
proposals and failures remain recorded. Passing these finite checks does not
prove general correctness. Lab, Oracle and Evolve name execution, review and
revision roles; they need not be separate agents.

\hypertarget{evolve}{}
\textbf{2. Experiments and limitations.}
\textbf{Continuation learning and counterexample refinement.}
Access histories $h$ and continuation tests $t$ index an observation table,
$H_{h,t}=f(ht)$, for deterministic observations $f$. Given exact field-valued
answers and a square full-rank table, a counterexample supplies a history--test
pair whose nonzero residual increases the rank~\cite[Proposition 1, pp.\ 2--3]{weighted}.
Our repair preserves the paired access history and distinguishing test required
by this construction. It does not discover the model class. Finite challenges
replace the global equivalence oracle, so a completed fit establishes agreement
only on those challenges. The smallest
queue example fits in ten source executions and agrees on lengths 0--32. Its five
rational-linear coordinates are relative to the imposed class: one nonlinear
integer counter represents that queue more simply.

\hypertarget{trace}{}
Across eight inspected native configurations, both the repaired refinement and
a generic bank of one-symbol questions complete every fit. Generic questions
cost 11,379 acquisition source executions versus 11,395. Later finite checks retain errors:
UTF-8 scores 4,903/4,913; SQLite rowid 190/192 and AUTOINCREMENT 191/192.
These are diagnostic fixtures, not eight independent applications.

A separate six-teacher study holds cached answers identical. Retaining acquired
questions reduces extension queries by 11.2--63.1\% in six declared conditions.
Yet every joint lifetime costs 5,427--5,698 source executions, versus 3,071 for
separate ordinary modules. Blindly transferring all learned suffixes into the
simpler learner costs 3,541 and hurts five of six targets. Exact certificates
there concern supplied rational-linear references, not arbitrary native software.
Useful questions have not yet made the full architecture economical.

\hypertarget{results}{}
\textbf{Reusable SQLite testers.}
Five authored batch clients supply development experience. Two generated testers
then run unchanged on five account-transfer clients, four faulty and one correct.
Each has six query slots per client; invalid and duplicate queries consume slots.
Target execution finishes before a separate 400-case panel opens.

not adaptive testing or independent transfer. Charges exclude engineering and
host costs; these populations are not pooled with the other studies.

\textbf{Separate investigation diagnostic.}
On twelve parent episodes from SQLite, LRU and UTF-8 contracts, repair without
new investigation improves confirmation from 259/576 to 365/576 answers.
Fixed testing scores 364, compiled investigation 354, a direct batch 339,
feedback-aware two-turn requests 343 and outcome-masked requests 331.
The paired feedback gain is twelve answers on one parent; eleven parents tie.
Only five pairs reach a second decision. Eleven parent programs are unique,
but there are only three authored mechanisms; case rows are not independent
replications. No investigation arm beats repair alone. Ling3.1Flash on DeepInfra
uses 130 calls for \$0.12982944. Separate qualification, failed host admission
and a funding-only continuation retain their own receipts; no failed attempt
is erased or counted twice.

\hypertarget{research}{}
\textbf{3. Related work and research directions.}
We propose to retain distinguishing tests, their supporting histories and
observations, revised programs, and procedures for selecting informative tests
on subsequent tasks. Selective transfer between representation learning and
test generation remains unimplemented. The current contribution is an
implementation repair and reproducible comparisons with negative controls.

\textbf{Related work.}
ILF trains a testing policy from symbolic-execution traces~\cite[\S\S4--5]{ilf};
Zenith does not train model weights. CoverUp guides a fixed LLM with source,
coverage and execution feedback to generate regression tests~\cite[\S3]{coverup};
coverage is not fault detection. Voyager retains executable skills within a
fixed acquisition loop~\cite[\S2]{voyager}.
STOP recursively rewrites its code-improvement scaffold~\cite[\S4]{stop}.
Hyperagents edits task and meta-agent code, then evaluates a fixed acquired
meta-agent in another domain. Its reported cross-domain compounding comparison
does not establish a statistically significant advantage~\cite[\S\S5.2--5.3]{hyperagents}.
These precedents motivate comparisons with ordinary program and test reuse;
Zenith has not established an advantage over these alternatives.

\textbf{Evaluating an acquired improvement procedure.}
Let an initial improver produce a successor. Freeze each improver while it edits
identical untouched starting solvers on fresh families. Select children on
development evidence, freeze their identities, then evaluate once on held-out
inputs; never select the best test score. Keep the evaluator outside the edits
and count acquisition, failures, evaluation, execution and human intervention.
Ablate the learned questions while preserving observations and saved code.
Only then compare reinvesting the successor against continuing the original
improver. Any gain remains bounded by the tested tasks and budgets.

\textbf{Mathematical evaluation tasks.}
The \href{https://github.com/wcook04/plectis-erdos}{Plectis mathematical corpus}
contains Lean proofs and open obligations. Its argument-continuation exporter
identifies candidate prerequisite theorems and remaining obligations.
\href{https://github.com/wcook04/plectis-atlas}{Plectis Atlas} presents OpenAI's
mathematics release and attributed outside assessments. Neither is a demonstrated
learning integration with Zenith. We would need action, outcome, cost and target
relevance records; finished proofs omit alternative attempts. Lean checks supplied
proofs; failed search does not establish falsehood. Mathematics is a possible
harder transfer domain after cheap software tests.
