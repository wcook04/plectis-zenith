\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Zenith: learning from a reasoning graph's mistakes}\\[4pt]
Will Cook \quad\textbullet\quad 12 October 2026
\end{center}

{\small\textbf{This is not financial advice.} This paper describes experimental
research. Historical examples and model outputs are not investment
recommendations and must not be used to make financial decisions.}

\hypertarget{idea}{}
We have a network of reasoning nodes. Each node is given some evidence and
produces an analysis or prediction that later nodes read. Can that procedure
learn from its mistakes? The aim is efficient forecasting across market regimes.
The example below tests an execution repair, not forecasting skill.

Take the actual saved feeds at an earlier time. Let \textbf{Lab} read them,
choose the assets worth predicting and save its predictions. Then open the
later snapshot. \textbf{Oracle} compares the predictions with what happened
and proposes an explanation. \textbf{Evolve} proposes a change to the
procedure. Test the changed and unchanged versions on the same later inputs
before deciding what to keep.

The model weights stay fixed. We change a node's instruction, its incoming
context or its connections. The hard part is whether the change improves a
later prediction, rather than just making an old mistake easier to explain.

Successive version comparisons draw on AlphaGo Zero~\cite{alphago}; bounded

Here the histories are finite and externally generated.

\textbf{Start with the data we already have.}
The local archive contains the original six feeds: stocks, ETFs, macro series,
Stockgrid, prediction markets and news. A calculator derives cross-asset
summaries from those inputs. For the worked replay we use the saved
20 and 21 July 2026 snapshots~\cite{feeds}. Each contains all seven artifacts. The earlier
stock payload has 457 entries; 456 stocks have numeric prices in both snapshots.

This is a retrospective replay executed now with a current hosted model.
Its training data may contain later information. We are testing the operation
of the procedure on saved data, not recreating what that model could have
known in July or establishing a trading advantage.

\textbf{A snapshot can contain a history.}
A point-in-time packet may contain a state (a ``photo'') or dated histories
(a ``video'' up to the cutoff). Either can target a later state; forecasting
a future path needs its own horizons and loss. Future work would use authorised
proprietary snapshots with verified availability times and revision vintages.
Richer histories do not remove missing information or look-ahead risk.

\hypertarget{trace}{}
\textbf{Trace before changing.} Was useful evidence absent, overlooked, grouped
badly or misinterpreted? Save the first node where the claim lost support.
A repeated error motivates a test; a persuasive explanation does not prove the fix.

\hypertarget{research}{}
\textbf{Research direction: learn how each node does the same job.}
A shared neural controller could read market conditions available at the
cutoff and choose a few semantic settings for each reasoning node. Each
setting in $[0,1]$ selects a small wording variation in one prompt component.
The node's purpose, inputs, work budget and output contract stay fixed.
This mechanism is proposed, not implemented or validated by the example.

\textbf{A small semantic change, not a different task.}
The Polymarket miner still reads seed state and its feed and extracts the same
kind of ranked signals. A component at $0.1$ might say ``consider sentiment as
one explanation''; at $0.2$, ``give sentiment slightly more interpretive weight
where supported.'' Factual measurements, ranking rules and required checks
remain fixed. Other nodes have their own bounded interpretive components.
Neither a high setting nor a high market indicator makes a claim true.

{\small \textbf{What can be trained.} Search discrete settings, or train the
numerical controller from measured losses. Ordinary backpropagation requires
local derivatives (see
\href{https://github.com/karpathy/micrograd/blob/7bc720e951fe422b8f8814aa5aa1b64121d26b4c/micrograd/engine.py}{micrograd});
hard text lookup and hosted LLM calls expose no such path.
\href{https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/blob/f1260ff14610286cb295c7d3a5ee2cbfd0ba5cb9/dspy/teleprompt/mipro_optimizer_v2.py}{DSPy}
provides a prompt-search reference. The base LLM stays fixed. VIX and sentiment
are incomplete regime features; robustness to every situation is not assured.
Detailed proposal and source references: \path{docs/research-direction.md}.}

\hypertarget{results}{}
\textbf{A measured failure and one change.}
Four full Lab attempts used original snapshots from 26 June, 13 July, 20 July
and 21 July 2026. All used DeepSeek V4.1 Flash through DeepInfra, the same graph
and policy, complete feed data, and a 4,096-token response limit. Each completed
the inventory and seven miners, then stopped when Cross-correlation's JSON
response ran out of tokens~\cite{windows}.

We added one instruction at that node: keep the four required arrays, at most
three records per array, at most four subjects per record, and short summaries.
We froze the instruction after inspecting the two earlier episodes. On the two
later inputs, the changed node received exactly the baseline miners' saved
outputs and went through the native validator:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Later input & Original instruction & Changed instruction\\
\midrule
20 July close & truncated at 4,096 & valid JSON, 1,308 tokens\\
21 July open capture & truncated at 4,096 & valid JSON, 1,172 tokens\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both changed responses still exceeded the requested four-subject bound: their
largest records had nine and eleven subjects. The native schema permits this.
The measured gain is local execution validity, with incomplete instruction
compliance. It says nothing yet about prediction quality.

\textbf{What to measure next.}
Complete Lab first. Then measure selection and forecast error separately:
which assets were selected, coverage, their frozen predictions and later
observations. When scoring the whole universe, fix the same fallback for
unselected assets. Use a proper score such as quantile loss for quantile
forecasts~\cite{scores}; abstention must not become a zero-loss win.

The later windows overlap, the analyst had already seen the July 20 failure,
and there is one model sample per arm. These are not blind, independent trials.
Collection times establish availability of the saved files, not the clocks
of their underlying price bars. Until those clocks are verified, price changes
are differences between saved values, not a clean financial backtest.

An agent authored and tested this execution repair. The full Oracle/Evolve
loop has not run, and improved forecasting remains untested.
