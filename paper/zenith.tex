\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Zenith: programs, experiments and useful memory}\\[4pt]
Will Cook \quad\textbullet\quad 15 October 2026
\end{center}
\hypertarget{idea}{}
\textbf{Abstract.}
Zenith asks whether solving one problem can improve how the next is investigated.
It retains programs, measured evidence and questions that expose their limits.
Three separate mechanisms currently run: model-driven program repair, reuse of
generated testers, and an authored continuation learner. Repair alone improves
259/576 later answers to 365/576; added investigation does not beat it.
Two generated testers find three of four authored faults; fixed tests find all
four. Retained questions help in a controlled memory comparison, while the whole
learner still costs more than ordinary alternatives. These results motivate a
question about improving future improvement, without demonstrating a new general
learning method or recursive self-improvement. We describe the missing connection,
a possible mathematics application and the comparisons needed to test them.

\textbf{1. Programs, evidence and questions.}
The retained object is $(P,E,Q)$: a program, source-bound observations and
executable tests or distinguishing continuations. A useful addition must improve
later behavior or reduce the work required to acquire it. Cached answers,
learned representations and better investigation procedures require separate
controls. Model weights remain fixed in these studies.

\hypertarget{lab}{}
\textbf{Find what needs to change.}
A wrong answer may reflect discarded history, an incorrect use of sufficient
state, or behavior never observed. The archived SQLite program retained pending
inserts but ignored them when reading allocation state; another rows-only helper
really did discard necessary history. These need different interventions.
The records expose the distinction; they do not establish reliable autonomous
diagnosis. A model's explanation of a failure remains a hypothesis.

\hypertarget{oracle}{}
\textbf{The implemented loop.}
In the native study, a model receives a task contract, a whole candidate program,
examples and the measurements available to its condition. It proposes queries;
the host compares candidate and reference answers. A repair request proposes
replacement code. Selection preserves every previously correct measured case
and the aggregate development result before later panels open. Rejected
proposals and failures remain recorded. Passing these finite checks does not
prove general correctness. Lab, Oracle and Evolve name execution, review and
revision roles; they need not be separate agents.

or disappear when simpler code works better. Its existence is not a result.

\hypertarget{evolve}{}
\textbf{2. What the experiments establish.}
\textbf{Question memory and learner repair.}
A continuation asks what a source returns after further actions. Two histories
with the same current output can have different future consequences. An authored
rational-linear learner uses answers to selected continuations as coordinates,
then fits transition and readout maps. Predictive state representations and
weighted automata learning are established precedents~\cite{psr,weighted};
Zenith does not discover this model class.

The earlier learner sometimes retained a distinguishing question but discarded
the access history witnessing it. Keeping both repairs this defect. The smallest
queue example fits in ten source calls and agrees on lengths 0--32. Its five
rational-linear coordinates are relative to the imposed class: one nonlinear
integer counter represents that queue more simply.

\hypertarget{trace}{}
Across eight inspected native configurations, both the repaired refinement and
a generic bank of one-symbol questions complete every fit. Generic questions
cost 11,379 acquisition calls versus 11,395. Later finite checks retain errors:
UTF-8 scores 4,903/4,913; SQLite rowid 190/192 and AUTOINCREMENT 191/192.
These are diagnostic fixtures, not eight independent applications.

A separate six-teacher study holds cached answers identical. Retaining acquired
questions reduces extension queries by 11.2--63.1\% in six declared conditions.
Yet every joint lifetime costs 5,427--5,698 source executions, versus 3,071 for
separate ordinary modules. Blindly transferring all learned suffixes into the
simpler learner costs 3,541 and hurts five of six targets. Exact certificates
there concern supplied rational-linear references, not arbitrary native software.
Useful questions have not yet made the full architecture economical.

\hypertarget{results}{}
\textbf{Reusable SQLite testers.}
Five authored batch clients supply development experience. Two generated testers
then run unchanged on five account-transfer clients, four faulty and one correct.
Each has six query slots per client; invalid and duplicate queries consume slots.
Target execution finishes before a separate 400-case panel opens.

Both generated testers repeat one constant query, ignore feedback and spend ten
slots on duplicates. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on Morph uses 46 calls for \$0.007117764,
including development and compilation. All three sequential versions, including
two earlier unsuccessful attempts, cost \$0.019295552 across 133 calls.
The family was inspected during engineering. This establishes executable reuse,
not adaptive testing or independent transfer. Charges exclude engineering and
host costs; these populations are not pooled with the other studies.

\textbf{Separate investigation diagnostic.}
On twelve parent episodes from SQLite, LRU and UTF-8 contracts, repair without
new investigation improves confirmation from 259/576 to 365/576 answers.
Fixed testing scores 364, compiled investigation 354, a direct batch 339,
feedback-aware two-turn requests 343 and outcome-masked requests 331.
The paired feedback gain is twelve answers on one parent; eleven parents tie.
Only five pairs reach a second decision. Eleven parent programs are unique,
but there are only three authored mechanisms; case rows are not independent
replications. No investigation arm beats repair alone. Ling3.1Flash on DeepInfra
uses 130 calls for \$0.12982944. Separate qualification, failed host admission
and a funding-only continuation retain their own receipts; no failed attempt
is erased or counted twice.

\hypertarget{research}{}
\textbf{3. A direction to test, open to correction.}
The candidate connection is to retain the experiment that reveals a missing
distinction, the histories and outcomes justifying it, the revised program, and
a procedure for recognising when a similar investigation would help elsewhere.
Selective, economical transfer between representation and investigation remains
unbuilt. The current contribution is a repaired learner and reproducible
comparisons with informative negative controls.

\textbf{Relation to earlier work.}
Learning tests or reusable procedures is already studied in
ILF~\cite{ilf}, CoverUp~\cite{coverup} and Voyager~\cite{voyager}.
STOP~\cite{stop} applies a code improver to itself;
Hyperagents~\cite{hyperagents} makes the task agent and its improvement machinery
editable. Self-editing, a graph or fixed foundation-model weights cannot establish
Zenith's novelty. A contribution would require a specified mechanism linking
measured distinctions to better subsequent improvement, and evidence beyond
these neighbours and strong ordinary alternatives.

\textbf{Testing improvement of the improver.}
Let an initial improver produce a successor. Compare both on untouched copies
of the same starting solvers and fresh task families under matched lifetime
budgets. Then compare reinvesting the successor to produce another generation
with continuing the original improver. Keep the evaluator independent of edits;
count failed proposals, acquisition, evaluation, execution and human intervention.
Remove the learned questions or procedure while retaining the same observations
and saved code. Better final answers alone do not show that the improver became
better. Any positive result would be bounded by the tested tasks and budgets,
without establishing open-ended acceleration.

\textbf{Why mathematics might help.}
The \href{https://github.com/wcook04/plectis-erdos}{Plectis mathematical corpus}
contains Lean proofs, partial results and open obligations. Its argument-continuation
exporter identifies candidate theorems for a required hypothesis and remaining
obligations. This suggests decisions to study: prove a prerequisite, try another
route, or seek a counterexample. \href{https://github.com/wcook04/plectis-atlas}{Plectis Atlas}
helps readers inspect OpenAI's mathematics release and attributed outside
assessments; the mathematics and assessments belong to their named authors.
Neither project is a demonstrated learning integration with Zenith.
A useful record would connect what was known, the action, measured outcome,
cost and effect on the target. Finished proofs do not supply missing histories
of alternatives. Lean checks a supplied proof; failed search does not establish
falsehood, and correct lemmas need evidence of relevance. Begin with cheap
software tests; mathematics is a possible harder transfer domain.
