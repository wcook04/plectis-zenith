{\Large\bfseries Zenith: evaluating revisions to a reasoning graph}\\[4pt]
Will Cook \quad\textbullet\quad 12 October 2026
\end{center}

{\small\textbf{Experimental research prototype.} The public example uses synthetic observations of a disclosed toy generator. No predictive improvement has been demonstrated.}

\hypertarget{idea}{}
\textbf{Abstract.} Zenith studies forecasting a changing, partially observed
system through heterogeneous, incomplete and delayed observations. Lab freezes
predictions; Oracle evaluates later evidence; Evolve proposes one revision for
a held-out matched comparison. The synthetic fixture prepares the first request
with zero model calls, no completed forecast and no demonstrated improvement.

\textbf{1. Evaluation protocol.}
Reasoning reconciles meanings and clocks, distinguishes missing from conflicting
evidence, proposes competing explanations and selects explicit forecast targets.
The DAG records computational dependencies, not physical causality or independent
votes. Keep the model, targets, score and budget fixed during comparison.

\textbf{Synthetic observations.}
Five source perspectives describe the same abstract System A: measurements,
subsystems, environment, events and interventions. A sixth derives summaries
from their supplied values. The disclosed generator uses dimensionless values,
seed 17 and the prescribed recurrence
$x_{n+1}=0.7x_n+0.2c_n+u_n+\epsilon_n$, with seeded noise.
This is a toy construction, not a learned model or physically validated simulation.
Provenance and receipts identify the inputs~\cite{demo}.

\textbf{Physical motivation.} Sensors expose different aspects of a changing
state; history may recover information absent from one instant, but some state
can remain unobservable~\cite{tedrake}. Heat-transfer observations could support
a six-hour temperature target; position, acceleration and force histories could
support a one-second vibration target. Known forces, boundaries and interventions
condition the forecast. Proposed reasoning aligns clocks, units and source
dependence, compares sensor-fault and state-change explanations, and selects an
appropriate physical model or numerical tool. Solver integration is unimplemented
and untested; narrative cannot substitute for equations. System A remains a
separate toy. Match entity, target, horizon and later truth.

Collect runs with the graph fixed; count unique cases across overlapping
windows. Freeze one revision before opening comparison outcomes. Cases used to
choose it become development data. The fixture does not establish that reasoning adds value over direct or numerical
baselines.

\textbf{Prior work.} DSPy composes LM modules~\cite{dspy}; MIPRO optimises their
instructions and examples against a metric~\cite{mipro}. AlphaGo Zero~\cite{alphago}
and autoresearch~\cite{autores} motivated successive comparisons. Their results do not establish a forecasting advantage here.

\hypertarget{trace}{}
Diagnosis separates evidence absent from the packet, evidence omitted from a
node's inputs, unsupported reasoning, incorrect prediction and execution
failure. Keep unresolved cases unknown. Post-outcome research may explain an
event but cannot become earlier evidence. Recorded interventions and repeated
failures do not by themselves identify causes.

Evolve proposes a bounded instruction, context or dependency change. Compare
original and revised graphs on the same further cases with the model and budget
fixed. Report selection coverage, forecast error, failures and cost separately,
with a score suited to the declared forecast~\cite{scores}. A selected subset
may look accurate while missing useful targets; declare how abstentions count.

\hypertarget{research}{}
\textbf{3. Proposed context-conditioned prompts.}
A shared neural controller would use variability, missingness, disagreement and
event density available at the cutoff to select small text variants for each
node. Its outputs are \emph{prompt settings}: numbers in $[0,1]$ indexing a
versioned catalogue, distinct from learned controller weights. The node's task,
permitted inputs, output contract and work budget stay fixed. This extension
has not been implemented or evaluated.

Development is separate: measure configurations on development cases, fit or
select the controller, then freeze it before later testing. Search a small
catalogue first, following discrete prompt optimisation~\cite{mipro}.
Backpropagation could train the numerical controller from measured losses;
hard text selection and hosted LM calls expose no ordinary end-to-end derivative.
ProTeGi~\cite{protegi} and TextGrad~\cite{textgrad} use verbal feedback as a
gradient analogy. A critique suggests a revision; it is not a derivative or
proof of benefit. Nearby settings need not behave smoothly or monotonically.

\hypertarget{results}{}
\textbf{Verified boundary and next evaluation.}
The native graph loads the six synthetic artifacts and prepares the Seed request
with zero model calls~\cite{demo}. There is no completed CP1, CP2, forecast, Oracle diagnosis
or Evolve comparison. Historical results belong to their original configurations.
Complete Lab, score the frozen predictions against later observations and compare
one revision on further matched cases. Test any controller against fixed prompts
under the same information and budget, including regressions and uncertainty.
Synthetic preparation establishes neither real-world predictive performance nor
universal reliability. Detailed protocol and controller choices remain in
\path{docs/research-direction.md}.
