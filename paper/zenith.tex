\begin{abstract}
Financial language can describe substantial growth while revealing a weaker
outlook than an earlier disclosure. A useful reasoner must recover that
comparison, identify its economic channel and distinguish it from a return
still available after entry. Zenith develops this capability through dated
evidence, an inspectable change representation and feedback on subsequent
outcomes. Its implemented development route compares direct reasoning with an
interpretation--forecast graph, and separates reasoning from numerical
calibration. On 33 previously examined A-share development dates, two fixed
statistical controls had positive outcome alignment but excessive forecast
magnitudes. Scaling fitted only on earlier matured predictions reduced their
mean squared errors below a zero-relative-return reference by approximately
0.7--0.8\%. This modest, design-exposed result does not establish an LLM advantage.
Four newly compiled guidance-update cases, drawn from six original issuer
releases, supply richer comparisons, including mixed revisions and unchanged
revenue guidance alongside higher margins. The model comparison and learned
reasoning experiment remain unexecuted. The research hypothesis is that
feedback improves the recognition and use of financial changes enough to
improve later decisions beyond fixed reasoning, retrieval and calibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{The financial reasoning problem}
Salesforce's May 2023 release left fiscal-2024 revenue guidance at
\$34.5--34.7 billion while raising non-GAAP operating-margin guidance from
27\% to 28\% \citep{salesforce2023march,salesforce2023may}. A representation that
reports only positive earnings language misses the distinction between a
revenue revision and a profitability revision. Adobe's June 2023 release
requires another distinction: some annual targets rose while Digital
Experience revenue targets fell relative to their last published ranges
\citep{adobe2022december,adobe2023march,adobe2023june}. These are observable
changes in issuer guidance. Neither example supplies analyst consensus or
establishes an unexpected improvement from the market's perspective.

Even a correct comparison leaves a second question: what return remains
available at the intended entry? A release after the close may affect the next
opening price. A forecast for buying at that open must concern subsequent
continuation, reversal or delayed transmission. Work on headline-based return
prediction distinguishes price response from later predictability
\citep{lopezlira2023}; Zenith treats the remaining effect as something to measure.
Economic links can motivate indirect transmission \citep{cohen2008}, but a
shared name, news tag or semantic similarity does not establish such a link.

The proposed learning contribution is specific: experience should improve the
system's representation and use of financially meaningful changes, with later
decision gains beyond a strong fixed reasoner and numerical adaptation. This
claim is untested. The work reported here establishes an executable development
route, a measured calibration diagnosis and a concrete source-rich replacement
task.

\section{Separating interpretation from payoff}
Let $E_t$ contain evidence available by the decision time and $X_t$ contain
admissible numerical state. Zenith's graph produces an interpretation
$G_t=R_\theta(E_t,X_t)$. Its current contract asks for supported facts, change
relative to earlier evidence, an economic channel, what may already be priced,
and unresolved questions. References must identify supplied evidence. The
forecast node receives both $G_t$ and the original evidence, so a compressed
interpretation cannot silently become its only source.

The intended numerical interface is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat y_t=b_\phi(X_t)+h_\psi(G_t,X_t),
\end{equation}
with the baseline and incremental map fitted from earlier matured examples.
This makes two failures distinguishable: a poor representation and an
excessive payoff assigned to a useful representation. The present adapter
implements a small special case: an economic-change signal in $[-1,1]$, one
chronologically fitted nonnegative coefficient capped at 0.05, and a zero
baseline. An unknown signal remains null in the recorded output; the numerical
system applies a declared zero correction. A scalar cannot preserve every
dimension of a mixed event. The richer textual representation remains
available to the forecast node and for inspection.

A strong direct comparator receives the same evidence, numerical state,
decision clock and target, with instructions to perform the relevant
comparisons itself. The graph adds an intermediate representation and a second
call. Graph composition is established prior art \citep{got2024}; its additional
cost must earn a measured benefit. This initial comparison is matched on inputs
and requested model/provider, not on total tokens or calls.

Temporal price--text conditioning and financial instruction tuning appear in
Yu et al.\ \citep{yu2023temporal}. Zenith adopts the need for dated mixed evidence;
it does not reproduce their return bins, fine-tuning or reported results.
Huang et al.\ \citep{huang2026semantic} distinguish retrieving candidate
cross-stock relations from judging them against disclosure evidence. This
motivates separating issuer relevance from a hypothesized indirect channel.
Their graph construction and portfolio rule are not imported. These mechanisms
require source-grounded review: valid JSON with valid reference identifiers
does not prove that its claims follow from the text.

\section{What the existing forecasts reveal}
The A-share development panel uses ten issuers and 41 admitted issue dates in
2023 from the pinned TraderHarness archive
\citep{traderharness2026,traderharnessdata2026}. After eight initial fit dates,
expanding chronological controls produce 330 forecasts across 33 dates.
The numerical control uses trailing market state, evidence metadata and issuer
identity. A matched text control adds character 2--4-gram TF--IDF with at most
8,192 features. Transformations and ridge coefficients use earlier matured
training outcomes; the ridge penalty is one. This modest target-trained text
comparison is motivated by Ke et al.\ \citep{ke2019}, without reproducing their
estimator. It uses scikit-learn \citep{sklearn2011}.

The target is gross stock wealth return from the next open through the fifth
session's close, minus the matching CSI 300 price-index return. It is a forecast
target, not the return of an executable hedged portfolio. Each date has equal
weight; within a date, each admitted issuer has equal weight.
For forecast $q$ and outcome $y$, the square-loss identity
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{MSE}(q)-\operatorname{MSE}(0)
 =\mathbb E[q^2]-2\mathbb E[yq]
\end{equation}
separates forecast energy from outcome alignment. The numerical control has
energy $0.00048579$ and alignment $0.00008124$; the text control has energy
$0.00056507$ and alignment $0.00008840$. Both align positively with outcomes,
but their magnitudes more than exhaust that alignment. The best unconstrained
scalar fitted retrospectively on all 33 dates is 0.1672 for the numerical
control and 0.1564 for text. These all-date fits diagnose overscaling; they are
not later-example predictions.

We therefore measured a chronological scaling rule. At each query date, it
fits one coefficient from complete earlier date groups whose outcomes have
already matured. With $n\geq4$ such dates,
\begin{equation}
 c_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
 \frac{\mathbb E_{<t}[yq]}{\mathbb E_{<t}[q^2](1+4/n)}.
\end{equation}
Otherwise, or when the denominator is zero, $c_t=0$. The factor $4/n$ supplies
a zero-centred penalty equivalent to four dates of observed signal energy.
Warm-up forecasts remain in the score as zero corrections. This calibration
design followed inspection of the original aggregate errors. It was not retuned
after observing the new results, but the whole comparison is exposed development
evidence, not an untouched assessment. Selection on evaluation results is
itself a source of optimism \citep{cawley2010}.

Relative to the numerical control, the raw text increment has energy
$0.00008824$ and residual alignment $0.00001165$; it also overshoots.
Applying the same chronological rule to that increment on top of the scaled
numerical baseline gives MSE $0.00108653$. Its near equality with the scaled
text control supplies no compelling evidence of a distinct text advantage.
The approximately 0.7--0.8\% reduction below zero is small, dependent on the
development design and not accompanied by a significance claim. It changes
the diagnosis from no usable alignment to positive sample alignment overwhelmed
by magnitude. It neither tests nor rejects the unexecuted LLM.

\section{Giving the reasoner the deciding evidence}
The A-share archive contains short headlines and announcement titles, often
without the earlier disclosure needed for a comparison. A bounded retrieval
patch added only five earlier comparison items to training inputs. Some issuer
tags refer to related listed companies rather than the forecast issuer. These
limits motivate a fuller-source recurring event task before optimization.

The replacement development set contains four guidance updates from Adobe and
Salesforce, supported by six original issuer releases. They were chosen
purposively for accessible current/prior disclosures before scoring their
returns. Table~\ref{tab:guidance} records source comparisons, not model answers.
The compiler checks 13 metric comparisons across these cases and keeps their
comparison oracle separate from the model request. It preserves range endpoints
and widths: an unchanged midpoint need not imply unchanged guidance.

Adobe target sections are extracted from retained PDFs. Salesforce inputs
contain selected annual-guidance values transcribed from visually reviewed
tables; other measures and reconciliation tables are not supplied to the model.
Current-site navigation and later editorial additions are excluded. This tests
reasoning over reviewed extracts, not autonomous full-report extraction.
SEC acceptance records cross-check the release clocks. The reconstructed
decision time is acceptance plus five minutes, with entry at the next regular
open. Retrieved issuer mirrors are not authenticated historical snapshots,
and exact identity with historical SEC exhibit bytes has not been proved.
These distinctions remain in the source manifest.

Price support comes from the pinned FinMultiTime archive
\citep{finmultitimedata}. Twenty completed sessions and five future sessions
exist for each issuer and SPY, with exchange holidays respected. The historical price
adjustment basis is unresolved, including distribution treatment. Consequently
the current task scores no returns. It asks whether the reasoner recovers
financial changes while retaining unknown expectations and mixed directions.
Four requests are compiled and held; none has a model answer. The price gap
does not prevent this representation test, but prevents a credible trading
result from these cases.

\section{Developing a base, then testing learning}
The next A-share batch contains an excluded compatibility date and six
development dates selected from their inputs. Each date requires one direct
call, one change-interpretation call and one graph-forecast call: at most 21
calls in total. The pilot verifies the actual contracts before comparison.
Rejected responses and provider failures remain recorded. Inspection will
ask whether outputs identify the right issuer and period, preserve revisions
and uncertainty, and reason about the effect remaining after entry.
Direct and graph forecasts receive the same chronological calibration rule;
post-warm-up comparisons also report the reference methods on exactly the
same dates. Six dates can diagnose behavior, not establish superiority.

Development may alter retrieval, prompts, tools, topology, model, event family
or horizon. Each materially different version retains its inputs, attempts
and design exposure. A frozen comparison fixes choices for that run; it does
not permanently restrict the product to two nodes or a single mutable field.
Future assessment must report its role in development honestly. Modern models
may remember historical events, so chronological input filtering alone does
not establish freedom from training contamination \citep{glasserman2023}.

Once the base demonstrates useful reasoning, feedback can propose changes to
recurring procedures: retrieve each metric's last publication, verify the
accounting period, inspect a countervailing segment, or decline an unsupported
surprise claim. Textual optimization and reflective search supply relevant
mechanisms \citep{textgrad2024,gepa2025}. Zenith already has native proposal
paths; GEPA's adapter interface is a candidate for connecting reflective search
to them. This hypothesis does not require a new general optimizer.

The decisive comparison holds numerical adaptation common between a fixed
reasoner and one whose procedures learn. Retrieved experiences form another
control, related to FinMem \citep{finmem2023}; persistent investment guidance is
related to FinCon \citep{fincon2024}. Zenith's implemented memory retrieval
combines forward-text similarity with numerical-state proximity. Wang and
Yeung \citep{wang2026network} also motivate numerical and narrative analogs,
but their financial-network indicators are different targets. Their reported
performance is not evidence for stock-return prediction here.

An outcome-only teacher and a teacher that additionally sees later reporting
can test privileged feedback \citep{vapnik2015}. Both must produce procedures
usable from later forward evidence. A compelling explanation of a past miss
is not by itself a transferable lesson. Neither future-news text similarity
nor output validation establishes explanation fidelity. Learning is useful
only if later behavior and decisions improve beyond the appropriate controls.

\section{From forecasts to decisions}
A trading claim needs a position-sizing rule, an executable reference asset,
cash and overlapping positions, entry and exit rules, distribution entitlements,
transaction costs and relevant market constraints. If positions are decided
before the opening price is observable, the rule cannot use that price while
assuming execution at the same price. Remaining returns must be measured after
the declared information and execution delay.

The existing A-share label mixes stock wealth and index price returns and
omits costs, taxes, lots and execution restrictions. The guidance cohort has
an unresolved price basis. Neither supports a profitability claim. The current
evidence supports a narrower conclusion: baseline forecasts can contain useful
alignment obscured by scaling, and recurring disclosures supply testable
financial distinctions absent from short titles. Whether LLM reasoning extracts
those distinctions reliably, whether feedback improves their later use, and
whether either changes feasible decisions remain empirical questions.

\paragraph{Reproduction and supporting evidence.}
The repository retains source pins, per-date measurements, compiled comparisons
and exposure notes. The reproduction map in \code{paper/README.md} links these
artifacts, the active commands, the spending boundary and implementation owners.
An earlier 2015 manual-rule pilot established
pipeline feasibility only. Its immutable results, provider accounting,
conditional formal model and routing tests remain supporting artifacts, not
evidence of learned financial reasoning.
