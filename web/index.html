What does a Brier score measure?

A forecast assigns a probability p to a binary outcome y. The Brier score is the average of (p − y)². A correct, confident forecast approaches 0; an incorrect, confident forecast approaches 1.

The constant 50% forecast scores 0.25 for every observation. A model must improve on this baseline before this experiment supplies evidence of useful forecasting.

What this small experiment can establish

This is a historical, reproducible comparison of simple forecasting rules. The sample is limited to 30 Dow Jones constituents in 2011 and does not represent the whole market or a current market regime.

A historical replay is weaker evidence than a prospectively frozen prediction. It does not establish investment performance, trading profitability, or a reliable future edge. Benchmark rules here are not claims about the capabilities of any current language model.
