# Dated forecasts and adaptive selection

Zenith currently measures dated price forecasts, not an investment strategy. A useful next study should preserve its frozen evidence, exact truth and complete attempt history, then evaluate a bounded selection procedure on later dates. A favorable score from a small historical pilot does not establish generalization or profitability.

## What the backend already enforces

`research/cohort.py::freeze` fixes candidates, model/provider identities, graph/policy/input/truth bytes, target support and the source authority hashes. Development target maturity plus `embargo_days` must precede the first holdout issue; a calendar week cannot straddle the splits. This is a chronological separation rule, not a general implementation of purged cross-validation.

`attach` retains imports, including unavailable and failed runs. `_compare` requires the complete expected candidate/issue/target support before returning scores. It averages target losses within each issue, then issues, and exposes paired candidate-minus-baseline issue losses. `evaluate` selects on development and seals the champion before final holdout assessment. The twenty-development-issue gate is an operational minimum, not a significance or power calculation.

`benchmarks/stocknet_native.py::prepare` records prior historical exposure. August outcomes and deterministic test-price benchmarks have already been inspected; renaming a study cannot make them unseen. Retrospective input cutoffs also do not establish the original availability date of revised or adjusted prices, or exclude a contemporary model's pretraining exposure to historical outcomes. See [learning design](learning-design.md), [native benchmark](../benchmarks/stocknet_native.py) and [cohort evaluator](../research/cohort.py).

The earlier incomplete pilot and its HTTP 429 support operational observations about compatibility, transport and accounting. Those pilot attempts provide no paired forecast comparison. Completed cohort measurements are recorded separately in [the evidence status](status.md) and [the paper's receipt export](../paper/study-evidence.json). Unknown charges and failed attempts remain part of the record.

## Which statistical tools fit

White's *A Reality Check for Data Snooping* (Econometrica 68(5), 2000, 1097–1126; DOI 10.1111/1468-0262.00152) tests whether any member of a comparison family beats a benchmark in expected performance. Section 2 explicitly treats forecast squared error and trading returns as different objectives. Its dependence-aware bootstrap requires assumptions about the loss sequence and resampling scheme. This suggests retaining the full paired loss matrix and every tried alternative; it does not certify arbitrary adaptive graph search or make twenty dependent issue dates sufficient. [Original paper, academic-hosted PDF](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/White.pdf).

Hansen's *A Test for Superior Predictive Ability* (JBES 23(4), 2005, 365–380; DOI 10.1198/073500105000000063) uses studentization and a sample-dependent null distribution to reduce sensitivity to poor, irrelevant alternatives. SPA could be appropriate for a larger, fully recorded forecast family. The publisher abstract was inspected; the full algorithm was not independently reproduced here. Neither SPA nor Reality Check is implemented in Zenith. [Primary publisher abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1198/073500105000000063).

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu's *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*, author version revised 27 February 2015 (published Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 2017; DOI 10.21314/JCF.2016.322), defines a selection winner's tendency to fall below the median out of sample. Sections 2.1–2.2 require the performance matrix across configurations; “in sample” means the selection set. CSCV swaps partitions and is a selection-stability diagnostic, not prospective forecasting chronology. It cannot be computed honestly from only the final winner or an incomplete pilot, and it does not remove information already exposed during guided search. [Author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf).

Bailey and López de Prado's *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*, author version 31 July 2014, adjusts a selected return Sharpe statistic for multiplicity, track-record length and non-normality. The sections on expected Sharpe and DSR, plus Appendix A.3, distinguish effective independent trials from raw trial counts. It is not a correction for relative price error, and provider-call counts are not independent investment trials. Using it would first require a separate frozen position, execution, cost and return-series protocol. [Author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

Purging and embargo are relevant when training labels or information intervals overlap evaluation dates. López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (2018), Chapter 7, is the primary method locator; his [Cornell lecture index](https://www.quantresearch.org/Lectures.htm) identifies modelling and backtesting lectures. The complete book algorithm was not verified in this review. For the current strictly earlier-development/later-holdout layout, Zenith's mature-target-plus-gap check is the implemented boundary. Any future rolling or inner split must explicitly check label intervals; a fixed gap alone cannot be called a complete purging implementation.

## Smallest useful next change

Extend the existing cohort procedure rather than add another evaluator. Freeze an earlier inner development window, a later inner selection window, the candidate budget, loss, coverage rule and selection rule. Freeze the entire chosen graph and selection procedure before a still-later outer assessment window. At each boundary enforce target maturity, declared information intervals and the embargo. Adaptive proposals may read earlier errors; they may not inspect the outer outcomes to choose the next candidate. This nested chronological procedure is planned, not implemented.

Add an append-only exposure history that references existing study, run and feedback hashes: dates/outcomes inspected, proposed and rejected graph revisions, pilots, models and failed attempts. Continue it across study names. It records known exposure; it cannot prove that a model's pretraining excluded market history. Later outer windows already inspected must be labelled retrospective; stronger prospective evidence requires predictions frozen before truth becomes available.

Keep the paired issue loss matrix and all coverage/failure denominators. Before adding uncertainty claims, declare the statistic and a dependence model. A sufficiently long date-block analysis should resample the same date blocks jointly across candidates, retain cross-asset dependence and report sensitivity to block choices. Short, nonstationary samples can make such inference unreliable; there is no universal issue count that cures this. Repeated fixed-baseline runs can separately measure response variability, without treating responses on the same date as independent market observations.

Predeclare target horizon, price basis and relative-error aggregation. Forecast improvement remains a forecast claim. Profitability would require a separately frozen tradable decision rule, timing, transaction costs, turnover, fills, financing and risk accounting; this note proposes no trading framework. Mechanical diagnosis can locate missing or invalid evidence. Attribution of an error to data access or reasoning remains a hypothesis until a controlled intervention isolates it.
